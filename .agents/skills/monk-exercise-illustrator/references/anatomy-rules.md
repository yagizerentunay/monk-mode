# Anatomy and pose review

Map broad muscle names to the actual visible heads/regions for the specific movement before prompting. Do not paint a whole group merely because a data label names it. Primary overlay: saturated red near `#E53935`; secondary: lighter muted red; inactive anatomy stays grayscale. Follow muscle boundaries and fiber direction rather than applying flat blobs. If the selected camera hides a target region, use a more informative camera; do not invent visible anatomy.

For flat medium-grip barbell bench press, pectoralis major is the primary visible target, with anterior deltoid and triceps brachii as secondary. Do not color posterior deltoid, biceps or forearm as target muscles. Choose a stable three-quarter camera with visible chest and at least one upper arm. The lowered explanatory pose has feet planted, head/upper back/hips supported, scapulae set, wrists over forearms, elbows moderately tucked, bar near mid/lower chest. The raised pose has the bar above the upper torso, controlled near-extension, stable shoulders and wrists. Avoid depicting unsafe wrist extension, extreme elbow flare, a floating foot, a tilted bar or asymmetric plates as correct form.

For another movement, derive poses from its own instructions and mechanics. `START` is the first explanatory pose rather than necessarily the wording in a third-party instruction list; label the phase explicitly in the manifest. In isometrics, the second frame may show bracing, contraction or grip detail. If published instructions conflict with a safe pose, flag the conflict and request an expert review. A generated image, automated check or general muscle map is not a technical exercise review.
